# Human Writing Standards

## Purpose

AI-generated text has recognizable patterns that erode trust and signal inauthenticity. Professional websites (Zed, Basecamp, Linear, Stripe, Apple) never exhibit these patterns.

This skill defines rules for writing that reads as genuinely human. If someone ran this copy through an AI detector, it should pass. More importantly, it should *feel* like a person wrote it.

---

## RULE 1: Banned Vocabulary

These words are statistically overrepresented in AI output. Using them signals "AI wrote this" to readers, even subconsciously.

### Never Use These Words

| Category | Banned Words |
|----------|--------------|
| **Formal intensifiers** | delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, beacon, pivotal, crucial, vital, paramount, intricate, meticulous, comprehensive, multifaceted, nuanced, intricacies |
| **Hype words** | revolutionary, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, game-changer, unprecedented, transformative, unleash, unlock, harness, leverage, synergy, disruptive |
| **Journey words** | embark, navigate, journey, adventure, explore (when metaphorical), dive into, delve into, venture into |
| **Filler transitions** | moreover, furthermore, additionally, consequently, thus, hence, thereby, whilst, amongst, nevertheless, nonetheless |
| **Empty adjectives** | robust, seamless, frictionless, holistic, dynamic, vibrant, bustling, myriad, plethora, extensive |
| **AI favorites** | underscore, underpin, showcase, foster, garner, bolster, spearhead, facilitate, elevate, curate, craft (as verb), tailor, streamline |
| **Corporate speak** | leverage, utilize, optimize, synergize, incentivize, operationalize, actualize |

### Replacements

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "delve into the features" | "look at the features" |
| "navigate the complexities" | "deal with" or "handle" |
| "leverage AI capabilities" | "use AI" |
| "seamless integration" | "works with" or "connects to" |
| "robust solution" | "reliable" or describe what it does |
| "holistic approach" | "complete" or cut entirely |
| "foster collaboration" | "help people work together" |
| "comprehensive suite" | "full set" or just list them |
| "utilize" | "use" |
| "facilitate" | "help" or "enable" |
| "craft a solution" | "build" or "create" |
| "curated experience" | just describe the experience |
| "streamline your workflow" | "save time" or be specific |

## RULE 2: Em-Dash Discipline

Em-dashes (—) are the #1 giveaway of AI-generated text. AI uses them excessively where periods, commas, or restructuring would be better.

### Hard Limits

- Maximum 1 em-dash per paragraph
- Maximum 2 em-dashes per page/article
- Never use em-dashes to chain 3+ thoughts
- Never use em-dashes for simple lists

### AI Pattern (BAD)

```
Your files—all your documents—organized and searchable—available anywhere.
```

### Human Pattern (GOOD)

```
Your files. All your documents. Organized, searchable, and available anywhere.
```

Or:

```
Your files, organized and searchable. Available anywhere.
```

### When Em-Dashes Are Acceptable

- A single parenthetical aside: "Our app—unlike competitors—processes everything locally."
- Dramatic pause (rare, use sparingly): "One tool instead of five."

### Alternatives to Em-Dashes

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| "private—truly private" | "private. Truly private." |
| "find it—if you can find it—buried" | Restructure: "good luck finding it buried..." |
| "docs—spreadsheets—slides—notes" | "docs, spreadsheets, slides, and notes" |
| "fast—really fast—loading" | "fast. Really fast." |
| "simple—yet powerful" | "simple but powerful" or "simple. And powerful." |

## RULE 3: Banned Phrases & Structures

### Opening Phrases to Never Use

- "In today's [digital age/fast-paced world/ever-changing landscape]..."
- "Whether you're a [X] or a [Y]..."
- "In an era where..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "Here's the thing:"
- "At its core..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "In the realm of..."
- "Nestled within..."
- "Picture this:"
- "Imagine a world where..."
- "Have you ever wondered..."
- "In today's competitive market..."

### Closing Phrases to Never Use

- "In conclusion..."
- "To sum up..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Moving forward..."
- "That being said..."
- "All in all..."
- "In summary..."
- "The bottom line is..."

### Sentence Structures to Avoid

| AI Pattern | Human Pattern |
|------------|---------------|
| "Not only X, but also Y" | "X. And Y." |
| "It is worth noting that..." | Just state the thing |
| "This serves as a testament to..." | Cut entirely |
| "This highlights the importance of..." | Cut or rewrite directly |
| "X plays a crucial role in Y" | "X matters for Y" or "X helps with Y" |
| "It goes without saying that..." | Then don't say it, or just say it |
| "Needless to say..." | Same—just say it or don't |
| "It's no secret that..." | Just state the fact |

## RULE 4: Punctuation & Formatting

### Semicolons

- Use sparingly
- AI-written text overuses them
- Prefer periods for separation
- Semicolons work for closely related independent clauses, but a period usually works too

### Colons

- Fine for introducing lists
- Don't use for dramatic effect in marketing copy
- Don't use in headlines: "Product: The Future of X" → "Product"

### Exclamation Points

- Maximum 1 per page of marketing copy
- Never in headlines
- Never more than one in a row (!!!)
- Only in genuinely exciting moments, not for fake enthusiasm

### Commas

- Don't stack adjectives excessively: "simple, powerful, intuitive, elegant" → Pick 1-2
- Oxford comma: Use consistently (either always or never)
- When in doubt, fewer commas = cleaner prose

### Quotation Marks

- Use for actual quotes only
- Don't use for emphasis (use italics instead)
- Don't use for "so-called" snark unless intentional

### Ellipses

- Use rarely
- Never for trailing off in marketing copy
- Three dots only (...)

## RULE 5: Sentence Variety

AI produces monotonous sentence patterns. Human writing varies.

### AI Pattern (BAD)

```
Our product helps you save time. It organizes your files automatically. 
It uses AI to search semantically. It keeps everything secure on your device.
```

### Human Pattern (GOOD)

```
Save time. Our product organizes your files automatically and searches 
by meaning, not just keywords. Everything stays secure on your device.
```

### Rules for Variety

- Mix short sentences with longer ones
- Start sentences differently (don't repeat "It" or the product name at every start)
- Use fragments intentionally: "Private. Fast. Yours."
- Vary paragraph length (1-4 sentences)
- Occasional one-word paragraphs for emphasis

### Sentence Length Guidelines

- Aim for average 15-20 words per sentence
- Mix in short punchy sentences (3-8 words)
- Break up any sentence over 30 words

## RULE 6: Specificity Over Vagueness

AI writing is vague. Human writing is specific.

### AI (Vague)

```
Our platform offers a comprehensive solution for project management 
with cutting-edge AI capabilities that help teams collaborate more effectively.
```

### Human (Specific)

```
Track tasks, deadlines, and who's doing what. AI suggests what to 
work on next based on your priorities. Teams of 5-500 use it daily.
```

### Be Specific About

- **What the product does**: Not "helps you" but "saves your files"
- **Who it's for**: Not "users" but "freelancers who juggle multiple clients"
- **The outcome**: Not "improve productivity" but "finish projects 2 days faster"
- **Numbers**: "50% faster" beats "significantly faster"
- **Examples**: Show a real use case, not abstract benefits

## RULE 7: Headers & Structure

### AI Patterns to Avoid

- Headers with colons: "Product: A Revolutionary Approach" → Just "Product"
- Headers that are complete sentences
- Excessive hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3 → H4 on a simple page)
- Numbered headers when bullets would do
- Headers starting with "How" or "Why" for every section

### Human Patterns

- Short, scannable headers: "How it works" not "How Our Product Works to Help You"
- Parallel structure in header sets
- Headers that could be navigation labels
- Mix of header styles (questions, statements, fragments)

---

## RULE 8: Avoiding AI "Tells" in Tone

### AI Writing Sounds

- Over-helpful: "I'd be happy to explain..."
- Over-formal: "It is important to consider..."
- Over-enthusiastic: "This exciting feature..."
- Hedge-y: "generally speaking", "in most cases", "it could be argued"
- Distant: "Users can..." instead of "You can..."

### Human Writing Sounds

- Direct
- Confident (but not arrogant)
- Occasionally incomplete
- Occasionally informal
- Personal ("we" and "you")

### Examples

| AI Tone | Human Tone |
|---------|------------|
| "Our solution is designed to help users efficiently manage their tasks." | "Manage your tasks faster." |
| "This feature allows you to search using natural language queries." | "Search by describing what you want." |
| "We believe that privacy should be a fundamental aspect of any application." | "Your data stays on your phone. Period." |
| "It is recommended that users consider..." | "Try..." or "We recommend..." |
| "The platform provides users with the ability to..." | "You can..." |

---

## RULE 9: Avoiding Filler & Fluff

### Words That Add Nothing

- "very", "really", "extremely", "highly" (usually)
- "in order to" → "to"
- "due to the fact that" → "because"
- "at this point in time" → "now"
- "for the purpose of" → "for" or "to"
- "in the event that" → "if"
- "has the ability to" → "can"
- "is able to" → "can"
- "make use of" → "use"

### Before/After

| Fluffy | Clean |
|--------|-------|
| "We are very excited to announce..." | "Announcing..." |
| "In order to get started..." | "To start..." |
| "Has the ability to process..." | "Processes..." |
| "Is specifically designed for..." | "Built for..." |
| "Provides you with the opportunity to..." | "Lets you..." |

## Quick Self-Check Before Publishing

Run through this checklist:

1. **Vocabulary scan**: Ctrl+F for: delve, tapestry, landscape, leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive, crucial, multifaceted, embark, journey, moreover, furthermore, utilize, foster, showcase

2. **Em-dash count**: More than 2 on the page? Rewrite.

3. **Opening check**: Does it start with "In today's..." or "Whether you're..."? Rewrite.

4. **Colon check**: Does the headline have a colon? Probably remove it.

5. **Vagueness check**: Can you replace "helps you manage" with something specific?

6. **Read aloud**: Does it sound like a person talking, or a press release?

7. **"The" count**: Too many sentences starting with "The [product]..."? Vary it.

8. **Enthusiasm check**: More than one exclamation point? Cut some.

## Before/After Examples

### Example 1: Product Description

**BAD:**
```
In today's fast-paced digital landscape, managing your projects has 
never been more crucial. Our platform offers a comprehensive, 
cutting-edge solution—leveraging AI—to help you seamlessly organize, 
track, and optimize your workflow. Embark on a journey toward 
streamlined productivity.
```

**GOOD:**
```
Track projects without the busywork.

See who's doing what, when it's due, and what's blocking progress. 
AI handles the status updates so you can focus on the work.
```

---

### Example 2: Feature Description

**BAD:**
```
Our robust search functionality leverages cutting-edge natural language 
processing to deliver a seamless, intuitive experience. This transformative 
capability allows users to navigate their data effortlessly, unlocking 
insights that were previously buried in the tapestry of their files.
```

**GOOD:**
```
Search by meaning, not keywords.

"That doc about the Q3 budget" finds it—even if "budget" isn't in 
the title. Describe what you're looking for. We'll find it.
```

---

### Example 3: About Section

**BAD:**
```
At [Company], we believe that innovation is paramount. In an era where 
efficiency and productivity are increasingly vital, it's crucial to 
choose tools that prioritize your needs. Our comprehensive platform 
ensures that your data remains secure—always—while fostering 
collaboration across your organization.
```

**GOOD:**
```
We got tired of clunky tools that promise everything and deliver 
complexity.

So we built something simpler. One place for your team's work. 
Secure by default. No training required.
```

## Reference: The Zed Standard

Look at how Zed (zed.dev) writes:

> "We think there's a better way to write code."

> "When you move the cursor or type a character, you should see pixels 
> on the next refresh of your display—every time."

> "If you want someone's perspective on a piece of code, it should be 
> as easy as sending a message."

Notice:
- No hype words
- Specific and concrete
- Short sentences mixed with longer ones
- Confident but not arrogant
- Occasional em-dash (one per section, not per sentence)
- No "revolutionary" or "cutting-edge" or "game-changing"

This is the standard.
